01 Will the eclipse be visible from the right bank of the Scheldt?

At maximum eclipse, the sun sits just 7.5 degrees above the horizon. I wanted to know if trees and buildings would block the view.

02 Yes, the eclipse will be visible from all of the spots I tested.

I checked the sightlines at five possible viewing spots. Any of these locations is suitable for viewing the eclipse. But are some spots better than others?

03 Obstructions at each viewing spot

I compared the sun's path against reconstructed skylines at each spot. Even from the worst viewing spot I tested, near Het Steen, the exciting part of the eclipse will still be visible.

04 How this was measured
01
Tracked the Sun

I calculated where the Sun would appear every minute during the eclipse and checked the results against NREL’s solar-position method.

02
Constructed the skyline

I used one-metre public lidar data to create a detailed picture of the ground, trees and structures visible from each viewing spot. New development not captured by the lidar scan was supplemented using OpenStreetMap data.

03
Identified the obstacles

I used building footprints and land-cover data to distinguish buildings and vegetation from water and open ground.

04
Tested each sightline

From each viewing spot, I traced 210 sightlines across the western horizon, reaching up to 10 kilometres. Each line records the highest obstruction, allowing for the Earth’s curvature and the slight bending of light through the atmosphere.
